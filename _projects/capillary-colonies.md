*Myxococcus xanthus* glides under a thin film of water.
The cells gather even when the colony is dilute, and the usual story of motility-induced phase separation does not account for that.
Each cell deforms the film, and those deformations attract neighbors.
The force is capillary.

A pair of cells under an otherwise flat film feels a long-ranged attraction.
A crowd is different.
The other cells hold the film up, and the attraction becomes short-ranged, in the same way a charge is screened in a plasma.
With Paul Robin, Frank Jülicher, and Ricard Alert I am writing the continuum theory that starts from this shared film and asks when the colony should aggregate.
The manuscript is in preparation.

Simulations of the same film are in progress, from an explicit meniscus and from the short-ranged force measured inside the crowd.
I am not putting those numbers on this page.
They belong in the paper, once the comparisons are finished.
