Welcome! I am Jairo, a physicist who wants to understand how life works — from the forces that sculpt a single cell to the collective decisions that let populations survive or disappear.

I am a PhD candidate in Physics at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I work with [Sascha Hilgenfeldt](https://hilgenfeldt.mechse.illinois.edu/).
My research sits at the boundary of biological physics, soft matter theory, and non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.
I draw inspiration from quantitative experiments, build theoretical models from first principles, and test them — not the other way around.

Living matter is fascinating precisely because it operates far from equilibrium and spans enormous scales.
I believe that uncovering the organizing principles behind these phenomena — the physics that biology actually obeys — is one of the richest open frontiers in science.

Before coming to Illinois, I studied physics at the [Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú](https://www.pucp.edu.pe/), then earned two master's degrees: one at the [Instituto de Física Teórica – UNESP](https://www.ift.unesp.br/) in São Paulo, Brazil, and one at the [University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/) through the [Perimeter Scholars International](https://perimeterinstitute.ca/training/masters-program) program at the [Perimeter Institute](https://perimeterinstitute.ca/), supported by the [SAIFR/IFT–Perimeter Fellowship](https://journeys.ictp-saifr.org/saifr-perimeter-fellowships/).
Feel free to explore my research, look through my publications, or reach out if you want to talk physics.
