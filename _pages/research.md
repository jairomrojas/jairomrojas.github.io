<section class="research-group" id="mechanics">
  <h2>Mechanics of form</h2>
  <p>How does a cell's material structure determine its shape?</p>
  <p>I use continuum mechanics to connect forces within epithelial cells to their deformation and to the energy used in models of tissues.</p>
  <p>Our <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17810">2025 preprint</a> studies how cell outlines change along the apical–basal axis using a three-dimensional shell model.</p>

<section class="research-group" id="collective">
  <h2>Collective behavior</h2>
  <p>Some bacteria assemble into groups while they are still dilute.</p>
  <p>I study how a shared liquid film can draw cells together and how that attraction changes within a crowd.</p>
  <p>I also ask what a population's position and shape can reveal about the ways individual cells search for resources.</p>
  <p>Fluctuations in birth, death, and motion can end a population even when its average density appears stable.</p>
  <p>I examine how space changes the path to that rare event.</p>

<section class="research-group" id="information">
  <h2>Information</h2>
  <p>How can a code become robust to errors while the code itself evolves?</p>
  <p>I study small models in which sequences and their code change together, with selection acting on what they express.</p>
  <p class="research-note">This work uses toy alphabets rather than the full biological genetic code.</p>
